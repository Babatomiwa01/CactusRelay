# CactusRelay · Business plan and launch campaign
20 October 2026 · Concept / pre-validation · All financials are assumptions in nominal RUB.

## Investment thesis
Derived from the prototype in 05-prototype.md: sell reliable, measurable surplus coordination to small produce retailers, beginning with tomatoes in a compact Kazan cluster. Kitchens collect; CactusRelay coordinates and records handoffs. A successful prototype interaction is not evidence of demand. No incorporated company, signed customer, partner, revenue or investor commitment is represented.

## Customer, product and competition
Buyer of the subscription: owner/operator of a 1–3 outlet produce shop. Beneficiary and counterpart: an independent kitchen able to purchase small lots and collect within a short window. Initial job: move eligible surplus with less daily coordination. Existing substitutes: markdown stickers, direct calls, messaging groups, donations and general surplus platforms. Interview users about these substitutes before asserting an advantage. Start with one crop and one cluster; expand only if retention and unit economics support it.

MVP: Russian-language mobile web interface, lot quantity and price, pickup deadline, reservation confirmation, actual-weight receipt, operator support and waste log. A locally approved eligibility and handling protocol precedes real transfers. No software decision certifies food as safe. Retailers retain eligibility responsibility; buyers inspect and may reject. Do not encourage unsafe food to enter circulation.

## Commercial model
Proposed monthly subscription ₽1,500/shop plus 5% completed GMV. Pilot buyers pay retailers directly; later payment processing requires a separate cost and compliance model. Core illustration: 10 kg/day × ₽60/kg × 26 days = ₽15,600 monthly GMV/shop. Platform revenue = ₽1,500 + ₽780 = ₽2,280. Retailer gross proceeds after platform fees = ₽13,320 before labour, tax, transport and displaced normal-price sales. Buyer delivery is not subsidised; demand may fail if collection cost exceeds savings.

Contribution assumption per shop-month: revenue ₽2,280 less variable support ₽400, hosting/messaging ₽100 and claims reserve ₽180 = ₽1,600 contribution (70.2%). These are budget assumptions, not quotes or measured costs. Target acquisition cost ₽6,000 produces 3.75-month contribution payback. Subscription alone contributes ₽820 at these variable costs. Fixed overhead at ₽800,000/month implies 500 active shops at full core contribution, before financing and tax. Do not equate contribution margin with company profitability.

## Market and expansion
No sourced local shop count was established. Proposed serviceable launch cohort is 100 qualified shops, not a market census: 100 × ₽2,280 × 12 = ₽2.736m annualised revenue capacity at core assumptions. A 1,000-shop multi-cluster scenario equals ₽27.36m; it is a scaling scenario, not TAM. Build a verified outlet census and competitor map before underwriting expansion.

## Proposed financing and gates
Seek ₽12m equivalent pre-seed, subject to investor eligibility and appropriate legal review. At ₽800,000 fixed monthly spend, the gross allocation is 15 months before revenue and contingencies; actual runway depends on timing. Allocation: product/engineering ₽4.8m; local operations and recruitment ₽3m; research, handling protocols and legal review ₽1.8m; reserve ₽2.4m. No valuation proposed.

Month 0–2: 15 retailer interviews, 10 buyer interviews, two-week measured baseline and protocol review. Month 3–4: eight-week feasibility pilot, including baseline. Month 5–9: test paid conversion, 30–50 shops and one repeatable cluster playbook. Month 10–15: target 100 paying shops only after gates. Hire product engineer, operations lead and part-time food-handling/commercial advisors; names and commitments are not established.

Stop or redesign if fewer than five repeat kitchen buyers, median shop effort >5 minutes/day, pickup completion <85%, <20% reduction in edible discards after adjusting for stock volume, or paid retention <80% at 90 days. These thresholds guide a small study; statistical uncertainty must be reported rather than concealed.

## Marketing campaign · “Ripe today. Relay today.”
Objective: recruit the 10-shop / 5-kitchen pilot, then test paid conversion. Customer-facing execution requires Russian localisation by a native speaker and local interviews. English creative here is investor-facing.

Retailer message: “A clear pickup before closing.” Kitchen message: “Today’s tomatoes. A confirmed collection.” Avoid guaranteed savings or claims that all surplus is safe. Creative system uses real produce imagery, green/paper typography, a tomato-red deadline and actual handoff receipts with permission.

Six-week proposed campaign, ₽180,000 cash budget (separate from ongoing staff time): owner visits and printed QR lot guides ₽60,000; kitchen outreach and tasting/handling demonstrations ₽40,000; locally approved messaging placements ₽30,000; landing-page creative/localisation ₽25,000; measurement and contingency ₽25,000. Channels are hypotheses, not booked media. No messages have been sent.

Week 1: interview and map qualified shops. Week 2: recruit kitchens and verify collection windows. Weeks 3–4: run one cluster with operator assistance and an explicit consent process. Week 5: show measured operational results with shop permission. Week 6: test the proposed subscription offer; do not substitute signups for willingness to pay.

Funnel assumptions: 60 qualified retailer contacts → 24 meetings → 10 pilot shops → 7 paying shops; at full campaign cost, pilot-to-paid CAC is ₽25,714, above the ₽6,000 steady-state target. This discrepancy is deliberate: pilot learning spend must not be portrayed as efficient scale acquisition. Scale only after referrals and repeatable outreach lower CAC. For kitchens: 30 qualified contacts → 10 trials → 5 repeat collectors. Report actual conversion by channel weekly.

Metrics: qualified-to-meeting rate, activation within 7 days, first completed pickup, weekly repeat collections, paid conversion, 90-day retention, CAC by cohort, staff minutes, rejected lots, net waste at retailer and kitchen, and gross margin. Use unique campaign codes and consent-based attribution; avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.

## Investor presentation strategy
Pitch.html is prepared for audiences including representatives of Y Combinator, Techstars, Dogpatch Labs and Blackrock Capital. These names identify intended audiences only; none is a partner, endorser or confirmed attendee. Do not equate “Blackrock Capital” with BlackRock, Inc.; the intended entity is unverified.

For Y Combinator: lead with direct user learning, density and paid retention. For Techstars: show operator support and a repeatable cluster playbook. For Dogpatch Labs: discuss entrepreneurship support and potential ecosystem learning without asserting a funding mandate. For Blackrock Capital: confirm entity and mandate, then discuss risk, governance and eventual cash generation. All conversations are proposed; this task prepares the pitch and does not contact investors.

## Risks and next diligence
Main risks: low lot value, insufficient buyer density, normal-sales displacement, recipient-side waste, unsafe handling, no-shows, retailer churn and price sensitivity. Controls: small geographic scope, buyer collection, explicit handoff/rejection logs, no impact credit before confirmed collection and locally reviewed protocols. Current Russia-related investment, payments, data and trade constraints must be reviewed by qualified local and investor counsel before any commercial or investment commitment; no legal conclusion is offered here. Validate current competitor pricing, outlet counts, supplier costs and currency translation before fundraising.

Sources and evidence boundaries: see 03-problem-statement.md. All operational, pricing, market-size and financing numbers above are proposed assumptions. No local waste percentage, environmental conversion factor or fabricated traction is used.
