# CactusRelay · solution design

Input: the narrow, unvalidated coordination problem in 03-problem-statement.md.

## Offering and provider
Proposed provider: CactusRelay, a locally operated private startup. A lightweight Russian-language web service pairs shop surplus tomato lots with nearby verified kitchen buyers; buyers collect using their existing staff. CactusRelay supplies onboarding, reusable lot labels and a handoff log. No owned delivery fleet, cold-chain claim or automated safety certification.

Workflow: retailer checks eligibility under an approved handling protocol → posts weight, price and collection deadline → kitchen reserves → retailer confirms → both record actual collected weight with a one-time pickup code. Expired lots leave the available queue. Unsafe, mouldy or uncertain lots are rejected; condition and transfer records support recall handling. Commercial deployment requires locally qualified review of food handling, payments, privacy, contracts and cross-border constraints.

Proposed pricing: ₽1,500/shop/month plus 5% of completed surplus sales. Buyer pays retailer directly during the initial pilot; commissions are reconciled from confirmed transfers. No fee on uncollected lots. Proposed pilot: 10 shops, 5 kitchens, eight weeks; paid conversion is tested after baseline.

## Differentiation to test
Purpose-built tomato lots with explicit cutoffs, actual-weight receipts, buyer collection and waste measurement. Alternatives include markdowns, calls, messaging groups and general surplus marketplaces. The initial defensibility hypothesis is repeat buyer density and trusted handoff records; it is not a proven moat. Do not claim novelty or superior performance without comparison.

The cactus symbol signals resourcefulness. English investor concept; production customer interface requires Russian localisation and interviews.
