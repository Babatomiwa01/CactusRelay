# Problem statement · 20 October 2026

## Evidence from the research question
UNEP’s Food Waste Index 2024 estimates 1.05 billion tonnes of food waste in 2022 across households, food service and retail, including inedible parts. This establishes global scale, not a Kazan tomato market estimate. [UNEP report](https://wedocs.unep.org/items/dbe2cd4c-8384-4636-8359-5847f42b9711).

UC Davis describes tomatoes as sensitive to maturity, temperature and handling; indiscriminate chilling is not a universal solution. This supports testing coordination alongside appropriate handling, rather than promising extended shelf life. [UC Davis tomato facts](https://postharvest.ucdavis.edu/ar/produce-facts-sheets/tomato).

FAO documented Russian organisations joining SAVE FOOD in 2017. That is historical evidence of institutional interest, not a current partner commitment or proof of unmet demand. [FAO](https://www.fao.org/europe/news/detail/Russian-partners-join-SAVE-FOOD-aim-to-reduce-food-loss-and-waste/en).

## Narrow segment and diagnosed opportunity
Initial segment: owner-operated Kazan produce shops with 1–3 outlets, daily tomato stock and no integrated surplus reservation system, within a proposed 3 km pilot cluster. Buyer segment: independently operated kitchens purchasing small same-day tomato lots and able to collect before closing.

Working problem statement: these shops may lack a reliable way to identify, offer and hand off edible same-day tomato surplus before its sale window closes. Repeated phone calls, uncertain pickup and low lot values may make redistribution uneconomic. Neither the frequency nor magnitude is locally verified. The opportunity is to test whether a simple lot-and-deadline workflow can recover value with less coordination effort.

## Validation before investment
Interview 15 shop owners and 10 kitchen buyers; weigh waste for two weeks across 10 shops. Recruit 5 collection-capable kitchens. Proceed only if at least 6 shops report recurring eligible surplus and 5 buyers commit to scheduled collection. Eight-week feasibility target: ≥20% lower edible discard versus adjusted baseline, ≥85% pickup completion, median staff time ≤5 minutes/day. These are decision thresholds, not results. Record displacement of normal sales and waste shifted to kitchens; report uncertainty and shop-level variation.

Limitations: brief desk research; no interviews, pricing tests, local market census or causal results. The assumed coordination bottleneck could be outweighed by low supply, food-safety constraints or buyer demand.
